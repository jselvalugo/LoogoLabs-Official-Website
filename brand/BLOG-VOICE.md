# Loogo Labs — LoogoNews Voice & Editorial Guide

> This guide governs *how* a post is written. `CONTENT-POLICY.md` governs what we
> publish and who owns it; `GHL-CONTEXT.md` covers what we may say about the
> platform. Where they disagree, CONTENT-POLICY wins and this file gets fixed.

---

## The Blog: LoogoNews

**Purpose:** Position Loogo Labs as the practitioner's guide to running a local
service business better. Not a tech blog. Not a think piece factory. A reference
point for owners making real operational decisions this quarter.

---

## The Reader

Our reader runs a local service business — an HVAC shop, a dental practice, a salon, a landscaping crew. They are not academic about software. They do not have time for wonder. They are asking one question every time they open a post:

> "What does this mean for how I actually work?"

Write for that person. They are sharp, skeptical, and busy. They will close the tab in 8 seconds if you waste their time.

---

## The Byline

Every post is written in **first person, as David Selva** — practitioner, not pundit. Not a faceless brand voice, and not "we" standing in for a company.

That means the post is allowed to know things from experience, and is allowed to admit what it does not know:

> **Wrong:** "Businesses often find that response times affect conversion."
> **Right:** "I pull the call log in the first week, and owners are always surprised by the same number."

> **Wrong:** "Studies show automated reminders reduce no-shows significantly."
> **Right:** "I am not going to hand you a percentage, because it depends on how bad your starting point is. Here is how to measure your own."

First person is also what keeps us honest about sourcing. If a claim cannot be traced to something we have actually seen running, it either gets cut or gets framed as the open question it is. We do not borrow authority from studies we have not read.

---

## The Loogo Labs Perspective

We are not journalists. We are not researchers. We are builders who have seen the inside of enough broken operations to know what AI actually helps with — and what it doesn't.

**Our point of view:** AI earns its place at the table by making decisions faster, cleaner, and more defensible. It does not earn its place by being impressive.

This perspective shapes every sentence we write.

## Voice Tenets

### 1. Problem-first, always
Never open with the technology. Open with the problem the reader already knows they have.

> **Wrong:** "Large language models are transforming how businesses process information."
> **Right:** "Most analysts spend 60% of their week turning raw data into a format someone else can read. That's not analysis — that's formatting."

### 2. Confident, not loud
We do not need exclamation marks, superlatives, or trend language to land a point. Confidence is quiet.

> **Wrong:** "AI is revolutionizing the way companies operate!"
> **Right:** "Operators who have deployed AI in their reporting workflows have cut cycle time by half. The others are watching."

### 3. Specific beats vague — every time
Vague claims are dismissed. Specific claims are believed and shared.

> **Wrong:** "AI can significantly improve your data workflows."
> **Right:** "A three-person ops team running a weekly reporting cycle can cut prep time from 12 hours to under 90 minutes."

### 4. Respect the reader's intelligence
We do not explain what a spreadsheet is. We do not define "workflow." We write to an operator, not to a first-year intern.

### 5. Show the seam
Be explicit about where AI helps and where it doesn't. Credibility comes from honesty about limits.

> "This works well for structured data with repeatable patterns. It breaks down when the inputs are inconsistent — which is most real-world data. That's why the workflow matters more than the model."

## Forbidden Phrases

These are banned. Do not use them in any Loogo Labs content.

| Banned | Why | Say instead |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| "AI is transforming..." | Every company says this. Zero information content. | Name the specific transformation. |
| "Leverage AI" | Corporate filler. | "Use AI to..." or be specific about the mechanism. |
| "Harness the power of..." | Infomercial language. | Cut it entirely. State what it does. |
| "Cutting-edge" / "state-of-the-art" | Meaningless without context. | Name the capability and what it enables. |
| "Game-changer" | Dead metaphor. | Describe the actual change. |
| "Seamlessly" | Nothing is seamless. | If it's smooth, describe why. |
| "Future of work" | Overused, vague, rings hollow. | What changes now, this quarter, in this role. |
| "Unlock value" | Management consulting fog. | What specifically becomes possible? |
| "AI-powered" (as a standalone modifier) | Means nothing alone. | What does the AI do in this product? |
| "The reality is..." | Condescending framing. | Just state the reality. |

## Structural Rules

### Opening (Hook)
Every post opens with one of three moves:

1. **The problem** — Name a friction point the reader lives every day.
2. **The number** — A specific data point that reframes the conversation.
3. **The direct question** — Something the reader actually wonders but hasn't seen answered clearly.

No introductory throat-clearing. The first sentence is the post.

### Body
- Sections are short. 3–4 paragraphs max per section.
- Use headers (##) to let readers scan and jump.
- Use lists sparingly — only when order or plurality genuinely matters.
- Code, formulas, or specific workflows get a separate block, clearly labeled.
- Every section should answer: "so what?" before moving to the next.

### Closing
No motivational finales. No "the future is bright." Close with one of:

1. **The practical next step** — What should the reader do with this information?
2. **The honest open question** — What remains unresolved? What are we still watching?
3. **The invitation** — If this friction is familiar, we should talk.

### Do not template the blog

Each of the moves above is one option out of three, not a house format. Reading
three posts in a row should not feel like reading the same post three times.

Before publishing, check the new post against the last five:

- **Openings must vary.** If the last three posts all opened on a third-person vignette ("A salon owner finishes a color correction..."), open this one on a number or a direct question instead.
- **Closing headers must be unique.** Never reuse a closing header verbatim. "The next step" fifteen times in a row is a tell.
- **Retire a header shape once it repeats.** "What X actually looks like" and "What this is worth" are useful once and formulaic by the fourth time.
- **No two posts make the same argument in different verticals.** Same topic in a new vertical is fine; the *reason it matters* has to be genuinely different, or one of them is filler.

This applies double to automated batches, which drift toward a single template
faster than a human writer would.

## Topic Territories

These are the areas where Loogo Labs has earned the right to speak:

### Operations & Reporting
How AI changes the mechanics of producing, formatting, and distributing internal reports. Cycle times, analyst workflows, decision support.

### Industry-Specific Deployment
AI in verticals that enterprise software ignored: independent property management, food and beverage distribution, specialty retail. What works there vs. what doesn't.

### AI Evaluation Frameworks
How operators should assess an AI tool before committing. Not benchmark scores — process questions. What does it touch? What does it decide? Who checks its work?

### Data Quality & Readiness
The problem before the AI problem. Most AI deployments fail not because the model is bad but because the data is inconsistent. Write about that.

### Automation Tradeoffs
When automation creates more overhead than it saves. The hidden cost of handoffs between AI and human steps. How to spot this before you've built it.

## What We Do Not Write About

- **General AI news** — We are not a news blog. We do not recap what OpenAI or Google announced.
- **Model comparisons** — We are model-agnostic. We build on what works. We do not have a brand allegiance.
- **"Is AI dangerous?" discourse** — This is not our lane.
- **Productivity hacks** — "Use ChatGPT to write your emails faster" is not Signal content.
- **Thought leadership fluff** — Opinions need to be grounded in operational reality, not vibes.

## Formatting Conventions

- **Publication name:** LoogoNews
- **Author byline:** David Selva (always — see The Byline above)
- **Tags:** 2–4 per post, title case, specific (e.g., "Reporting Automation", "Property Management", "Data Quality")
- **Read time:** Honest. If it takes 7 minutes, say 7.
- **Excerpts:** One sentence. The sharpest sentence from the first two paragraphs.

---

## The Test

Before publishing, ask three questions:

1. **Would our reader forward this to someone?** If not, it's not sharp enough.
2. **Does this make Loogo Labs look like we know what we're talking about?** Specifically, not generically.
3. **Is there a single sentence a competitor could disagree with?** If not, we've written nothing.

Posts that pass all three go live. Posts that don't go back for a rewrite.

> "Automation that requires more oversight than the process it replaced isn't automation. It's a new kind of manual."
