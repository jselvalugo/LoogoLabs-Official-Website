# LoogoNews — Content Policy

## Originality

**Every word on LoogoNews is written by us. Nothing is copied from anyone.**

Not one sentence of any post is lifted, verbatim or near-verbatim, from another website, article, newsletter, book, social post, competitor's blog, or press release. We write every post word for word, from a blank page, out of our own experience running these systems for clients.

This is absolute, and it rules out the softer versions of copying too:

- **No verbatim reuse.** Not a paragraph, not a sentence, not a distinctive phrase — regardless of whether the source is credited, obscure, or unlikely to notice.
- **No spinning or rewording.** Taking someone else's post and changing the words is still taking someone else's post. If the structure, argument, and examples came from their page, it is their page.
- **No borrowed authority.** We do not cite statistics, studies, or "research shows" claims we have not read in full at the source. If we cannot stand behind a number ourselves, it comes out or gets rewritten as the open question it actually is.
- **No unedited machine output.** Drafting tools are fine. Publishing what one produced, as-is, is not — see *How We Write* below.
- **No recycled text between our own posts.** Repeating ourselves across verticals is padding, and readers notice.

What we publish instead: what we have actually seen in the businesses we work with. That is the only source that needs no attribution, and it is the reason a post is worth reading in the first place.

### How we verify it

Before a batch publishes, we check the drafts against every post already live for repeated sentences and overlapping phrasing, and we read any claim that sounds like it came from somewhere else. A post that trips either check goes back for a rewrite rather than a citation.

If you ever find something on our site that you believe is yours, tell us. We will take it down the same day and correct the record publicly. We would rather be embarrassed once than be the kind of publication this policy exists to prevent.

## Ownership & Copyright

Because we wrote all of it, we own all of it — which is what makes the next part ours to give away.

Our content is copyrighted — automatically, the moment it is written, whether we want it or not. That is how copyright works everywhere; there is no box to leave unticked. What we *can* do is give it away on purpose, and we do.

Everything we publish is released under **CC BY 4.0**: anyone may copy, republish, translate, or build on it, commercially included, as long as they credit Loogo Labs. See `LICENSE-CONTENT.md` for the full terms. Everything we write is meant to be read, shared, and learned from. If it helps a business owner, it did its job.

Two things this does not license: our name and logo (`brand/`), and the site's source code. Those stay ours.

We will never gate content behind a form, a paywall, or an email capture. Posts are public and free — always.

## How We Write

Every post goes through a rewrite pass before it publishes. The goal is simple: it should sound like a person wrote it, not a tool. Specific rules:

- No AI-generated phrasing left untouched — every sentence gets read aloud and rewritten if it doesn't sound natural
- No filler phrases: "In today's landscape," "it's more important than ever," "leverage synergies," or anything a consultant would say
- Short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph. White space is not wasted space
- Write to a specific person — a salon owner, an HVAC operator, a real estate agent — not a generic "business owner"
- Use real numbers and real scenarios. Vague claims get cut
- If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough yet

## Cadence

We publish every day. One post per day, minimum.

Posts do not need to be long. A tight 400-word piece that solves one real problem beats a 2,000-word overview that covers nothing deeply. Depth over breadth, always.

If the day's post isn't ready, we still publish — even if it's a short operational note, a stat worth knowing, or a reframe of a question we heard from a client that week.
