# Brand Brain

Generic AI copy happens because the AI doesn't know the business. This skill fixes that once. It runs a short interview, builds a brand profile, and saves it so every later request (ads, posts, emails, pages) sounds like this brand from the first draft.

## 0. Check for an existing profile first

Look for `brand-profile.md` in the working folder, the project files, or anything the user attached.

- **Found** → read it, say in one line which brand you loaded, and offer to update a section. Don't re-interview.
- **Not found** → start the interview below.

Every other marketing task: if a `brand-profile.md` exists, read it before writing anything, and follow its voice rules and banned words.

## 1. Choose the fastest route

Ask which they prefer:

1. **Quick start (5 minutes)**: the 12 questions below.
2. **From what you already have**: they paste or attach their website copy, About page, a past post, a pitch deck or sales notes. You draft the profile from that, mark every gap, then ask only the questions you couldn't answer.

Route 2 is usually better. Offer it first if they mention a website or existing material.

## 2. The interview (ask in small groups, not all at once)

Group A: the business
1. Business name, and what you sell in one sentence.
2. Your main offer: what it costs and what the customer actually gets.
3. Where you operate: local area, country, online-only? Any language beyond English?

Group B: the customer
4. Who is your best customer? (role or type, situation, budget)
5. What problem pushes them to look for you, in their words?
6. What almost stops them from buying? (price, trust, timing, a past bad experience)

Group C: why you
7. Why do customers pick you over the alternatives? Name 2–3 competitors or alternatives.
8. Proof you can show: results, numbers, reviews, years, clients, certifications.

Group D: how you sound and look
9. Three words for how you want to sound, and one way you never want to sound.
10. Words, phrases or claims you never use (banned list).
11. Brand colors, fonts, logo notes; or "not decided yet."
12. The one action you want people to take (book a call, buy, visit, call, DM), plus the link or number.

Rules while interviewing:
- Keep the user's own words. Their phrasing of the customer's problem is gold for copy.
- If an answer is vague ("everyone", "quality service"), ask one follow-up for a specific.
- Never invent proof, numbers or reviews. Missing proof goes under **Gaps**.

## 3. Build the profile

Write `brand-profile.md` with exactly this structure:

```
# Brand Profile: [Business name]
_Last updated: [date]_

## Snapshot
One sentence: we help [customer] get [outcome] through [offer], unlike [alternative] because [reason].

## Offer
- Main offer, price, what's included
- Secondary offers (if any)
- Primary call to action + link/number

## Customer
- Who they are
- The problem, in their words (quote the user)
- What they want instead
- Top objections and the answer to each

## Positioning
- Category we compete in
- Alternatives / competitors and how we differ from each
- 3 messaging pillars (theme + one-line proof each)

## Proof bank
- Results, numbers, reviews, credentials (only what the user gave)

## Voice & tone
- Sounds like: [3 words]; never sounds like: [1–2 words]
- Sentence style (short/long, casual/formal, emoji yes/no, slang yes/no)
- Do / Don't table with a rewritten example for each row
- Banned words and claims

## Visual identity
- Colors (hex if known), fonts, logo notes, image style

## Channels
- Where the brand posts or advertises today, and where it wants to

## Gaps
- What's missing and why it matters (e.g. "No reviews yet: limits proof-based ads")
```

## 4. Show it working

After saving, prove the profile works with three short samples in the brand's voice:
1. A social caption (under 60 words) with the call to action.
2. A two-line ad (headline + primary text).
3. A one-sentence elevator pitch.

Then ask: "Anything that doesn't sound like you?" Fix the voice section based on their answer. A profile that has been corrected once is worth far more than one that hasn't.

## 5. Deliver

- Save `brand-profile.md` as a file the user can keep, and tell them to attach it (or keep it in the project) for future marketing requests.
- Also give a one-page **positioning statement** and the **voice guide** as separate short sections they can share with a designer or VA.
- End with the top 2 gaps to fill first.

## Guardrails

- Don't fabricate facts, testimonials, statistics or awards.
- Don't copy a competitor's slogans or wording.
- Keep the profile under ~2 pages; it's a working tool, not a brand book.
- When the business changes (new offer, new price, new audience), update the matching section and the date instead of starting over.
