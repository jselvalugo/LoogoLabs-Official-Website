# Campaign Brief Builder

Produce a one-page brief that a designer, a media buyer and a client could all work from without a meeting.

## 1. Read what you were given

The input can be rough: bullet notes, a pasted transcript, an email thread, a single sentence. Pull out every fact that answers a section below. Keep the user's own numbers and names exactly.

## 2. Fill gaps honestly

For each section, one of three things happens:

- **Stated** → use it.
- **Reasonably implied** → write it and mark it *(assumed)*.
- **Unknown and important** (budget, launch date, primary goal) → list it under **Open questions** instead of inventing it.

Ask the open questions at the end, not before you start. A draft with gaps is more useful than a list of questions.

## 3. The brief

Use exactly this structure:

```
# [Campaign name]

**One-line summary:** what we're doing, for whom, and why now.

## Objective
One primary goal, measurable. (e.g. "120 qualified demo bookings by March 31")
Secondary goals, if any.

## Audience
Who they are · what they want · what's stopping them · where they spend time.

## Key message
The single sentence we want them to remember.
Three supporting points.
Proof we can actually show.

## Offer & call to action
What they get and the exact action we want.

## Channels & roles
| Channel | Role in the campaign | Format | Owner |

## Budget
Total and split by channel. Mark (assumed) if not given.

## KPIs
| Metric | Target | Where it's measured |
Include one leading indicator and one outcome metric.

## Timeline
| Phase | Dates | What happens |
Prep → Launch → Optimize → Report.

## Assets needed
Checklist of creative, copy, landing pages, tracking.

## Risks
Two or three things most likely to go wrong and the plan for each.

## Open questions
Numbered, each answerable in one line.
```

## Rules

- Keep it to one page: if a section runs long, it belongs in a separate doc.
- One primary objective. If the user gives several, ask which one wins when they conflict.
- Tie every channel to the objective; cut channels that don't serve it.
- Tracking is an asset: list UTMs, pixels and conversion events under "Assets needed".
